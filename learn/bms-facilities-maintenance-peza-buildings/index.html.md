## BMS Scope in PEZA Buildings

In PEZA-registered buildings, a Building Management System (BMS) plays a crucial role in ensuring efficient facilities maintenance. The BMS scope typically includes monitoring and control of HVAC, lighting, elevators, life-safety, and security systems. In a PEZA-registered industrial park such as Laguna Technopark, the BMS typically holds HVAC to a temperature band the tenant leases against. The HVAC system is a critical component of the BMS, as it regulates temperature, humidity, and air quality. In a PEZA-registered BPO tower in Bonifacio Global City, the BMS monitors and controls the HVAC system to ensure a comfortable working environment for employees. The BMS also integrates with other systems, such as lighting and security, to optimize energy consumption and enhance occupant experience. What an HVAC repair costs in a PEZA building depends less on the fault than on when it was found: on a PM round, or by a tenant.

Lighting and elevator systems are also within the BMS scope. A word of caution on scope before the examples go further, because it decides which rules you are reading: **not every Philippine economic zone is a PEZA zone**. The Subic Bay Freeport Zone is administered by the Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority under RA 7227, and Clark sits under its own authority in the same way; PEZA operates under RA 7916 and its zones are separate. If your building is inside the Freeport, your locator registration, your reporting and your inspections run through SBMA, not PEZA. The maintenance practice in this guide applies either way; the paperwork and the inspecting body do not. The BMS also monitors elevator performance, including speed, load, and door operation, to ensure smooth and safe transportation of people and goods. At every handover, the shift in-charge reviews the BMS dashboard to ensure that all systems are functioning within normal parameters.

Life-safety and security systems are critical components of the BMS, as they ensure the well-being and safety of occupants. The BMS monitors and controls fire detection and suppression systems, as well as security cameras and access control systems. In a typical ecozone factory the BMS integrates with fire detection so that a confirmed alarm reaches occupants and emergency responders without waiting for someone to notice a panel. ## Tenant SLA Frameworks and Expectations

In PEZA-registered buildings, such as the ecozone factories in Cabuyao, Laguna, tenant SLA frameworks play a crucial role in ensuring that BMS and facilities maintenance are performed to a high standard. A well-structured SLA framework outlines the expectations and responsibilities of both the facility management team and the tenants, promoting a collaborative approach to maintenance. A framework of this kind sets response times per fault class rather than one blanket number: a trapped-passenger elevator call is measured in minutes, a failed lift in hours, a warm floor by end of shift. Whatever the tiers, write down who starts the clock, because that is what gets argued about later.

The SLA framework should also define the scope of work, including the frequency of preventive maintenance activities, such as monthly checks on HVAC systems, lighting, and life-safety equipment. In a mixed-use development running around the clock, the facility management team might perform daily checks on the building's security systems, with a supervisor on duty at all hours to oversee the process. Hive carries the checklist and the work order, so a 24/7 rota hands over a written record of what was checked rather than a verbal one.

When it comes to BMS maintenance, a typical SLA framework might include a monthly PM cadence, with activities such as checking and replacing air filters, inspecting electrical connections, and testing system controls. For example, at a PEZA-registered factory in Pampanga, the maintenance planner might schedule monthly PMs on the building's AHU-3 system to ensure optimal performance and energy efficiency. A mid-sized PEZA-registered building budgets BMS maintenance as a line of its own, separate from tenant fit-out work, because the two get confused otherwise.

Effective tenant SLA frameworks also require clear communication and collaboration between the facility management team and tenants. This might involve regular meetings and updates on maintenance activities, as well as a clear process for reporting issues and tracking work orders. The call that tests the framework is the one on the graveyard shift, when the day team is asleep and the tenant on the phone is a BPO floor that cannot stop. What matters then is that the in-charge can raise the fault against the right asset and the tenant can see it was raised. By setting clear expectations and promoting collaboration, PEZA-registered building owners and facility managers can ensure that BMS and facilities maintenance are performed to a high standard, supporting the needs of tenants and occupants.

In conclusion, a well-structured tenant SLA framework is essential for ensuring that BMS and facilities maintenance are performed effectively in PEZA-registered buildings. By outlining expectations and responsibilities, defining the scope of work, and promoting collaboration, facility management teams can ensure that maintenance activities are performed on schedule and to the required standards. The review that keeps this honest is the mid-afternoon one, before the shift turns over: what was raised today, what is still open, and what the next shift is inheriting.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the role of BMS in ensuring building safety?

BMS plays a critical role in ensuring building safety by monitoring and controlling various building systems, including life-safety and security systems. BMS equipment should be maintained regularly, ideally on a monthly basis, to ensure optimal performance and extend equipment lifespan. The Philippine Electrical Code (IIEE Code) recommends regular maintenance of electrical equipment, including BMS components.
