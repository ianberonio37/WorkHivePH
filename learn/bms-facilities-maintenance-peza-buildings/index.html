BMS Scope in PEZA Buildings

A building management system (BMS) monitors and controls a building's services. In a PEZA-registered building, that usually means five systems. They are HVAC, lighting, lifts, life safety and security.

HVAC matters most. It holds the temperature and humidity band a tenant leases against. Fire detection is next. A confirmed alarm must reach occupants and responders at once. Nobody should have to notice a panel first.

Check one thing first: is your zone a PEZA zone at all? Not every Philippine economic zone is. SBMA runs the Subic Bay Freeport under RA 7227. SBMA is the Subic Bay Metropolitan Authority. Clark has its own authority too. PEZA runs under RA 7916, and its zones are separate. Inside the Freeport, SBMA handles your registration, reports and inspections. PEZA does not. The maintenance practice here applies either way. The paperwork does not.

At every handover, the shift in-charge reviews the BMS dashboard. The goal is simple: every system inside its normal range.

Tenant SLA Frameworks and Expectations

An SLA is a service level agreement. It sets what the facility team owes each tenant. A good one sets response times by fault type, not one blanket number. Answer a passenger trapped in a lift within minutes. Fix a failed lift within hours. Cool a warm floor by the end of the shift.

Whatever the tiers, write down who starts the clock. That is what gets argued about later.

The SLA should also set the PM scope: what gets checked monthly, and who does it. Keep BMS maintenance as its own budget line, apart from tenant fit-out work. The two get confused otherwise.

The call that tests the SLA comes on the graveyard shift. The day team is asleep, and the tenant is a BPO floor that cannot stop. Then the in-charge must raise the fault against the right asset, fast. And the tenant must hear about it.

Monthly Preventive Maintenance Cadence

A monthly PM schedule in a PEZA-registered building covers, at minimum:

  • HVAC systems
  • lighting
  • lifts
  • life-safety systems, with routine tests of fire alarms and emergency lighting

In a building that never closes, run the tests at shift changes. That is the one moment the floor is reliably between tasks.

Energy is the easiest saving to defend. A 10 percent cut in consumption shows up in the same month's bill.

PEZA-Specific Compliance Requirements

Electrical work in the building follows the Philippine Electrical Code (PEC). Fire and life safety follow their own codes and inspections.

Energy falls under the Department of Energy, through RA 11285. It reaches a building by its annual energy use, not by its zone. So a Freeport locator under SBMA sits inside the same DOE rules. In practice, that means efficient lighting and HVAC, and records of what they used.

WorkHive keeps the maintenance part of that evidence: the PMs, the faults and who did what, with dates. It does not measure energy use. Your meters and BMS do that.

Best Practices for BMS and Facilities Maintenance

Find each fault before a tenant does. That is what a well-kept BMS and a steady PM round buy you.

Put intrusive tasks in a window when the building is truly quiet. A BPO tower on three shifts may have no quiet window at all. Then agree the schedule with the tenant. Do not assume it.

A planned repair avoids the premium of an unplanned one. That premium is the callout, parts at whatever price the supplier has that day, and the tenant's patience.

Finally, give one planner the BMS trends to read. Watch for air handlers that start hours before anyone arrives. That is the cheapest energy waste to find.

Frequently asked questions

What is the role of BMS in ensuring building safety?
BMS plays a critical role in ensuring building safety by monitoring and controlling various building systems, including life-safety and security systems. In the Philippines, the Department of Labor and Employment's Occupational Safety and Health Standards (DOLE OSHS) require building owners to implement safety measures to protect occupants.
How often should we maintain BMS equipment?
Monthly is the usual starting cadence, with life-safety tests on their own schedule. Follow each manufacturer's manual too. Electrical work must meet the Philippine Electrical Code.
What does WorkHive do for BMS and facilities maintenance?
WorkHive keeps the maintenance record around your BMS: faults against assets, monthly PMs with checklists, and who did what. It does not connect to the BMS itself. Report Sender can email the day summary to a tenant.
What are the PEZA-specific compliance requirements for BMS and facilities maintenance?
Follow the Philippine Electrical Code for electrical work, the fire and safety codes for life safety, and the DOE rules under RA 11285 for energy. Check your zone authority's own requirements too: PEZA, SBMA or Clark.
How do I train my BMS and facilities team?
Give them regular training on each system, and use the manufacturers' courses. The Society for Maintenance and Reliability Professionals (SMRP) offers the CMRP certification.
What are the consequences of non-compliance with PEZA regulations?
Non-compliance with PEZA regulations can result in penalties, fines, and even cancellation of PEZA registration. Building owners and facility managers must ensure that their BMS and facilities maintenance meet PEZA requirements to avoid these consequences.
