What is a Skill Matrix?

A Skill Matrix is a powerful tool used to track and visualize the skills and competencies of your maintenance team. In a Philippine plant setting, such as in Calabarzon's industrial zones, it helps identify skill gaps and training needs. For instance, a plant supervisor at a facility in Cabuyao might use a Skill Matrix to determine which technicians are qualified to work on specific equipment like Pump P-204B.

The matrix typically consists of a grid that maps employee names against various skills or tasks. This allows you to see at a glance who is proficient in which areas, such as electrical maintenance, mechanical maintenance, or welding. On a continuously manned plant like one in Subic, where operators work rotating shifts, a Skill Matrix ensures that the right personnel are assigned to tasks based on their competency levels.

Implementing a Skill Matrix can also help with workforce development and succession planning. For example, a maintenance planner in a Bulacan plant might use the matrix to identify potential candidates for senior roles based on their demonstrated skills and experience. By doing so, the plant can spend its training budget on TESDA NC II and NC III certification for the people whose gaps the matrix actually shows.

When tied to a competency scale: anything from a simple aware / assisted / independent ladder to WorkHive’s five named levels: a Skill Matrix becomes an even more effective tool. This scale helps assess an individual's level of proficiency in a particular skill, allowing for more targeted training and development. In a Davao plant, for instance, a shift in-charge might use this scale to evaluate a technician's ability to work independently on a specific task, such as maintaining Boiler B-1.

Mapping NCs onto the 5-Level Scale

In the Philippines, TESDA National Certificates (NC II, NC III) are widely recognized as a benchmark for technical skills. At WorkHive, we map these certificates to our Skill Matrix, a tool for the kind of plant that has to assess and develop a technical workforce - a Batangas refinery, a Laguna bottling line, a Bulacan food plant. The Skill Matrix scores each discipline on five levels, and every one of them is reached the same way: by passing that level’s 10-question exam at 7 of 10. A TESDA certificate is not an input the tool accepts: it is a good prediction of which exams a technician can already pass.

The five levels, and where a TESDA NC usually lands on them:

  1. Safety Awareness: knows the hazards and the isolation rules for the equipment, has not yet worked on it.
  2. Supervised Practice: does the work with a more experienced person present. Most NC II holders sit here on arrival.
  3. Independent Technician: does the routine work of the discipline alone. Most NC III holders sit here on arrival.
  4. Senior / Specialist: handles the non-routine faults and the equipment nobody else will touch.
  5. Engineer / Strategist: decides what the plant maintains and why, and trains the people who do it.

Safety Awareness is the floor, and it is a real level rather than a placeholder: a planner at a plant in Pampanga may know exactly how Pump P-204B is isolated and inspected without ever having done it, and that knowledge is worth recording before anyone is sent to the pump.

Supervised Practice is where an NC II holder usually starts: on the night shift at a plant in Calabarzon they work Boiler B-1 alongside a senior technician. Independent Technician is where an NC III holder usually starts: a shift in-charge at a PEZA-based plant in Bulacan who troubleshoots Conveyor #2 without calling anyone. The word usually is doing real work in both sentences: the certificate is evidence of training, and the exam is what moves the level.

The top two levels are where a national certificate stops being a useful predictor. An experienced electrician at a plant in Davao with an NC III often becomes the plant’s own instructor, taking junior technicians through AHU-3 at the afternoon changeover, but no NC says so, because Senior / Specialist and Engineer / Strategist describe what someone does in your plant rather than what they were assessed on. Those two levels are earned on the exams and demonstrated on the floor.

So a planner holding an NC II does not acquire a ‘Pump Maintenance’ badge; they sit the Mechanical exam and earn the badge for the level they pass.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between TESDA NC II and NC III?
NC II certifies a defined range of largely routine tasks performed to a standard, mostly under supervision. NC III certifies a wider and less routine range - diagnosing faults, working from incomplete information, and taking responsibility for the output of others. That last part is why several industries treat NC III as the entry requirement rather than as a bonus.
How do I get TESDA NC II or NC III certified?
You can get TESDA NC II or NC III certified by completing a TESDA-accredited training program and passing the certification assessment. Contact TESDA or a local training provider for more information.
Which level does a TESDA NC map to?
WorkHive scores each discipline on five levels: 1 Safety Awareness, 2 Supervised Practice, 3 Independent Technician, 4 Senior / Specialist, 5 Engineer / Strategist. A level is reached one way only - by passing that level's 10-question exam at 7 of 10. The tool has no way to import a certificate and no way for a supervisor to assign a level, so a TESDA NC does not place you on the scale. Read it as a prediction instead: an NC II usually means Levels 1 and 2 are within reach today, an NC III usually means Level 3. The exam is what confirms it.
How do I map TESDA NC II and NC III to my plant's skill matrix?
The mapping is a table you keep, not something the tool ingests. WorkHive gives each worker a private matrix across five disciplines - Mechanical, Electrical, Instrumentation, Facilities Management and Production Lines - and levels are earned by exam. So write down which discipline and level each TESDA qualification corresponds to in your plant, keep that table in your own training records, and let the exams confirm it worker by worker.
What are the benefits of TESDA NC mapping?
The benefits of TESDA NC mapping include improved worker competencies, increased productivity, and better workforce development. TESDA NC mapping also helps Philippine industrial plants comply with industry standards.
Is TESDA NC mapping mandatory in the Philippines?
TESDA NC mapping is not mandatory, but it is highly recommended for Philippine industrial plants. By mapping TESDA NC II and NC III certifications to your skill matrix, you can ensure compliance with industry standards and improve worker competencies.
