Mapping TESDA NC II and NC III to Your Skill Matrix

By WorkHive Editorial Team · 18 May 2026 · 10 min read
Short answer: A TESDA National Certificate (NC) proves trade skills. This guide covers NC II and NC III. They are a national scale, so they carry from one employer to the next. They also carry real training time. Electrical Installation and Maintenance NC II takes 402 hours of training. SMAW NC I and NC II take 268 hours each. WorkHive's Skill Matrix does not take a certificate as input. Read the NC as a forecast of which level exams a worker can pass.

What is a Skill Matrix?

A skill matrix is a grid of workers against the skills a plant needs. Each cell shows how well that person can do that work. It shows gaps at a glance, and where training money should go.

A matrix needs a scale. It can be as simple as aware, assisted and independent. WorkHive uses five named levels:

  1. Safety Awareness: knows the hazards and the isolation rules. Has not yet done the work.
  2. Supervised Practice: does the work with a more experienced person present.
  3. Independent Technician: does the routine work of the discipline alone.
  4. Senior / Specialist: handles the rare faults. Takes the equipment nobody else will touch.
  5. Engineer / Strategist: decides what the plant maintains and why. Trains the people who do it.

Mapping TESDA NC II and NC III to Your Skill Matrix

WorkHive scores five disciplines. They are Mechanical, Electrical, Instrumentation, Facilities Management and Production Lines. Each has the five levels above.

A TESDA NC does not enter the matrix by itself. There is no certificate field and no supervisor override. A worker reaches each level one way only. They pass that level's 10-question exam with 7 correct. So the certificate tells you which exams to expect a worker to pass. The exam records the result.

Each worker's matrix is private. Only the owner can read their skills, badges and exam results. A supervisor cannot open another worker's matrix. Nor can they place a badge.

Mapping NCs onto the 5-Level Scale

Here is where a TESDA NC usually lands:

  • NC II: most holders start at Supervised Practice, Level 2.
  • NC III: most holders start at Independent Technician, Level 3.

The word "usually" matters. The certificate shows training. The exam moves the level.

An NC II holder may work Boiler B-1 beside a senior technician. That is Level 2. An NC III holder may clear a Conveyor #2 fault alone. That is Level 3.

The top two levels are where a national certificate stops predicting. An NC III electrician may become the plant's own instructor. No NC says so, because Levels 4 and 5 describe what someone does in your plant. Workers earn them on the exams and show them on the floor.

Assessment-to-Badge Mapping

Know what a badge is before you map anything to it. WorkHive gives one badge per discipline per level. That is 25 in all: five disciplines times five levels. There are no topic badges for single machines.

So an NC II holder does not get a "Pump Maintenance" badge. They sit the Mechanical exam and earn the badge for the level they pass.

A supervisor's part is planning, not data entry. A supervisor can read an NC II as a sign. It says the Mechanical Level 2 exam is within reach this month. Then they make the time for it.

Machine-specific skill has no badge of its own. An NC III electrician may run Boiler B-1 on nights. Their NC III predicts the Electrical badge at Independent Technician. Their boiler skill stays in the plant's own records, beside the logbook entries that show the work.

Implementing TESDA NC Mapping in Your Plant

Start by checking which of your trades WorkHive covers. One of the commonest does not appear. There is no welding discipline, so an SMAW NC I or NC II has no column of its own. WorkHive scores a welder's maintenance work under Mechanical. The welding ticket itself stays in your training file.

Then write the mapping down as a table you keep. List which discipline and level each TESDA qualification matches. Each worker's matrix is private, so the shift in-charge works from that table at handover, not from the tool. Let the exams confirm it, worker by worker.

Review the table and the certificates every quarter. Review them again when a new unit like AHU-3 arrives.

Benefits of TESDA NC Mapping

The main gain is training money spent on real gaps. A plant stops paying to train people in what they can already do. The NC tells you where to expect skill, and the exam confirms it.

Workers gain too. The NC is a national scale, so it travels with them to the next employer. Some employers treat an NC III as an entry requirement, not a bonus.

No law requires this mapping. It does not make a plant compliant on its own either. It is a way to spend training time where the matrix shows the need.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between TESDA NC II and NC III?
NC II certifies a defined range of largely routine tasks performed to a standard, mostly under supervision. NC III certifies a wider and less routine range - diagnosing faults, working from incomplete information, and taking responsibility for the output of others. That last part is why several industries treat NC III as the entry requirement rather than as a bonus.
How do I get TESDA NC II or NC III certified?
You can get TESDA NC II or NC III certified by completing a TESDA-accredited training program and passing the certification assessment. Contact TESDA or a local training provider for more information.
Which level does a TESDA NC map to?
WorkHive scores each discipline on five levels: 1 Safety Awareness, 2 Supervised Practice, 3 Independent Technician, 4 Senior / Specialist, 5 Engineer / Strategist. A level is reached one way only - by passing that level's 10-question exam at 7 of 10. The tool has no way to import a certificate and no way for a supervisor to assign a level, so a TESDA NC does not place you on the scale. Read it as a prediction instead: an NC II usually means Levels 1 and 2 are within reach today, an NC III usually means Level 3. The exam is what confirms it.
How do I map TESDA NC II and NC III to my plant's skill matrix?
The mapping is a table you keep, not something the tool ingests. WorkHive gives each worker a private matrix across five disciplines - Mechanical, Electrical, Instrumentation, Facilities Management and Production Lines - and levels are earned by exam. So write down which discipline and level each TESDA qualification corresponds to in your plant, keep that table in your own training records, and let the exams confirm it worker by worker.
What are the benefits of TESDA NC mapping?
It points training money at real gaps, and the NC travels with the worker. It does not make a plant compliant on its own.
Is TESDA NC mapping mandatory in the Philippines?
No. No law requires it. It is a practical way to plan training: keep a table of which NC matches which discipline and level, and let the exams confirm it.
