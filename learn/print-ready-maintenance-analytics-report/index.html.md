## What is the WorkHive Analytics Report?

The Analytics Report is the printable output of WorkHive's Analytics Engine. It reads your logbook and PM completions. So a plant in the Laguna Technopark starts the month's review from a document. Nobody retypes figures the night before.

The KPI (key performance indicator) strip has four tiles. Each turns green, amber or red against its own threshold:

- **PM Compliance**, as a percentage
- **Fleet MTBF (mean time between failures)**, in days (the metrics guides work in hours)
- **Total Failures** for the period
- **High Risk (Predicted)**: the number of assets the risk model expects to fail next

Read the last tile first. It is the one a spreadsheet cannot give you.

## Who reads a maintenance report: managers, auditors, and plant heads

The plant manager reads it first. The head of operations and the reliability engineer read it too. In a plant here, the supervisor or planner makes sure someone acts on it.

Auditors can use it too, internal or external. It is a dated record of the period's figures and the plan. It does not prove compliance on its own, though. The records behind it do that.

## How the WorkHive Analytics Report Works

Any member of the hive can make one. The cover says whether a worker or a supervisor made it. You can type in your name and a title, such as "Plant Reliability Q2".

1. Open the Analytics Report page in WorkHive.
2. Pick a period: 30, 90, 180 or 365 days.
3. Tap Generate Report to build it from your records.
4. Read the summary, the KPI tiles, the action plan and the sign-off block.
5. Tap Save as PDF, and save it from your browser's print dialog.

## What Does the WorkHive Analytics Report Contain?

It opens with an **executive summary** of the period. Then come the four **KPI tiles**, coloured red, amber or green. These are plant-wide figures across the hive, not a card for one asset like Pump P-204B.

Next is an **AI action plan**, which names the assets to act on and the steps to take. Last comes a **sign-off block** with the names typed in. You print it and sign it by hand, so the signature sits on the same page as the figures.

**Worked example:** A plant manager runs the report for 30 days, then for 365. The short period shows this month. The long one shows whether PM compliance and failures are trending the right way.

## Implementing the WorkHive Analytics Report in Your Plant

Start where the data is. The report is only as good as the logbook and PM records behind it. So get the team logging every job, and closing PMs on time, before you hand the report upward.

Then make it a habit. Run it for 30 days at each month-end, read it with the supervisor, and sign it. Keep the PDFs, so you can check each month against the last.

## Frequently asked questions

### What data is required to generate the WorkHive Analytics Report?

Your logbook entries and PM completions. The more of both you keep, the more the report can say.

### Who can access the WorkHive Analytics Report?

Any member of the hive can make one; the cover says whether a worker or a supervisor made it. Plant managers, operations heads, auditors and reliability engineers are its usual readers.

### Is the WorkHive Analytics Report customizable?

You pick the period (30, 90, 180 or 365 days) and type in your name and a title. The figures and the plan come from your records and the AI, so you change them by fixing the records.

### Can I use the WorkHive Analytics Report for internal audits?

Yes, as a summary of the period. Pair it with the records it is built from, which an auditor will want to sample.
