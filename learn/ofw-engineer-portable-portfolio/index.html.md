## Turning Logbook Entries into Portable Evidence

As a Filipino engineer on an OFW track, building a portable maintenance portfolio is crucial for career advancement. Two separate records do this work, and it is worth knowing they are separate. The **Logbook** holds what you did: every maintenance task, from routine checks on Pump P-204B to a major overhaul of Boiler B-1, each with a date and your name on it. The **Skill Matrix** holds what you can do: the disciplines you have been assessed in and the level you reached. An employer wants both, and you export them from their own screens.

These Logbook entries are more than notes: they are evidence of hands-on experience, and the Logbook has its own **Export to CSV** that hands you the lot as `workhive-logbook-<your name>-<date>.csv`. It carries the equipment you worked on, the tasks you performed and the problems you solved. For example, you might have been called to Conveyor #2 at 02:30 on the night shift and had it running again by 04:10: the time, the fault and the fix, all on the record.

When it comes to overseas employment, a well-documented portfolio can make all the difference. Employers want to see proof of your experience and skills, not just claims. A portfolio built from your Skill Matrix and your logged work IS that proof: every claim points at an entry with a date and a name on it. A Filipino plant supervisor with a similar portfolio was able to negotiate a significant increase, because every claim on the CV pointed at an entry someone could open.

## What to Expect from a Portfolio Export

There is no single “portfolio” button. There are **three exports**, each from its own screen, and together they are your portfolio:

- **Skill Matrix → CSV.** Your skill record: primary discipline, each discipline you have been assessed in and the level you reached, with a note of the rule used to work the level out. Saves as `workhive-skills-<date>.csv`.
- **Logbook → Export to CSV.** Your entries: the date, the asset, the fault and what you did about it. Saves as `workhive-logbook-<your name>-<date>.csv`.
- **Resume Builder → .docx, .json or a cover letter.** The document you actually send. It fills itself from the skill badges you have earned, so passing a quiz puts the badge on your resume without you retyping anything.

A maintenance planner at a petrochemical plant in Calabarzon takes all three: the skills CSV says what they are qualified in, the logbook CSV shows what they did to the pumps and compressors that plant runs on, and the resume is what goes in the email.

The logbook CSV is the one that carries the detail an employer can check: if you have worked a continuously manned plant, each row reflects the date, the time and what was done: a routine inspection on the night round, an equipment handover at the shift change. It is a spreadsheet rather than a designed document, which is exactly what makes it verifiable; an interviewer can sort it by asset and see the pattern of work for themselves.

The skills CSV is the other half: a snapshot of what you have been assessed in and how far you got, which tells an employer where your strengths are and where the gaps still are. Note that it does not contain your logbook entries and the logbook export does not contain your skill levels: take both. A plant supervisor from Batangas, for instance, might have a portfolio that highlights their expertise in areas like predictive maintenance and reliability-centered maintenance, and can point at the failures that stopped recurring after they changed the strategy.

Overseas employers value a well-structured portfolio like this because it provides a verifiable record of an engineer's experience and skills. By including concrete data and information, such as equipment IDs, shift times, and cost savings, Filipino engineers can demonstrate their value and competitiveness in the global job market. For example, a shift in-charge from a Saudi refinery might look for a portfolio that showcases experience with similar equipment and processes, as well as evidence of proactive maintenance practices that minimize downtime.
