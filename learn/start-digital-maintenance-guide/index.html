Getting Started

How to start digital maintenance in a Philippine factory, step by step

By WorkHive Editorial Team · Published 2026-08-05 · 8 min read

To go from spreadsheets to digital maintenance, do four things in order. First, start a digital logbook, so every job is recorded the day it happens. Second, build an asset register on the ISO 14224 hierarchy, so every record is tagged to equipment. Third, turn recurring jobs into a preventive-maintenance schedule. Fourth, run a structured shift handover, so nothing is lost between crews. A small plant can do all four in about 30 days at zero software cost, and a preventive programme runs about 12–18% cheaper than running reactive (US Department of Energy / PNNL).

Who this is for

  • Maintenance supervisors moving a crew off paper and spreadsheets
  • Plant owners and plant managers with no budget for a CMMS
  • Maintenance technicians who record the job on the floor

Why digitise maintenance at all

A spreadsheet inherited from a departed engineer is a liability. Its formulas drift, its tabs multiply, and the one person who understood it is gone. Digital maintenance replaces that with structured, searchable, portable records. The payoff is a technician answering “when did we last replace this seal, and why?” in ten seconds instead of never. The US Department of Energy puts a preventive programme at roughly 12-18% cheaper than running reactive. Predictive maintenance saves a further 8-12%. That is why the steps below start with recording and scheduling.

Step 1: the digital logbook

Everything starts with capture. If a job is not written down the day it happens, the data is gone. A digital logbook takes 20 seconds per entry and immediately makes downtime, parts used, and repeat failures visible. The full rollout is in start a digital logbook in a Philippine factory. It includes how to get technicians to actually use it. For plants with spotty Wi-Fi, a logbook must work offline and sync later. That is a core WorkHive design choice.

Step 2: the asset register (ISO 14224)

A logbook is only useful if every entry is tagged to a specific asset. That means building an asset register: a hierarchy of plant → system → equipment → component. Follow the ISO 14224 taxonomy, so your data can be compared with industry benchmarks. You do not need a consultant or a budget. The step-by-step method is in building an asset register on zero budget. If the asset list already lives in a spreadsheet, do not retype it: in WorkHive a supervisor imports it on the CMMS Integration page as a CSV or Excel file, which is read in the browser (the file is never uploaded; only the rows you import are saved), and the same import takes PM schedules and spare parts.

Step 3: the preventive-maintenance schedule

With assets and history in place, turn the recurring jobs into a real PM schedule. Lubrication, inspections and filter changes each get a due date and an owner. Track completion as PM compliance: aim for the SMRP ≥90% benchmark. Start from proven free PM checklist templates rather than a blank sheet. The maintenance metrics guide shows how to prove the schedule is working.

Step 4: structured shift handover

Most plant knowledge is lost in the 5 minutes between shifts. A structured handover closes that gap: open work, abnormal conditions, pending parts. It is the cheapest reliability win available. Use the ready-made format in the maintenance shift handover template. In WorkHive, Shift Brain drafts the next shift's plan before sign-on (top risks, checks due, carry-over jobs, parts to set aside) for the supervisor to publish, and Report Sender emails the handover to the people you add by role. Voice capture in Tagalog or Taglish removes the “I’ll write it later” excuse: see voice-to-text on the plant floor.

The 30-day rollout

Week 1: logbook live, every job recorded. Week 2: asset register built for the critical line. Week 3: PM schedule loaded for those assets. Week 4: shift handover formalised and the first metrics reviewed. Do it on one critical line first, prove the value, then expand: a full-plant big-bang almost always stalls. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to buy a CMMS to start digital maintenance?
No. WorkHive is a free, offline-first maintenance platform. A small plant can run a digital logbook, asset register, PM scheduler and shift handover at zero software cost. The sequence and discipline matter more than the price of the tool.
What is the first step to digital maintenance?
Start a digital logbook so every job is recorded the day it happens. Without captured data, none of the later steps (asset register, PM schedule, metrics) has anything to work with.
How long does it take a small factory to go digital?
About 30 days, if you focus on one critical line. Logbook in week 1, asset register in week 2, PM schedule in week 3. Shift handover and the first metrics review in week 4. Then expand line by line rather than attempting the whole plant at once.
Why use ISO 14224 for the asset register?
ISO 14224 gives a standard plant-to-component hierarchy and failure taxonomy. Your reliability data is then structured consistently. It can be compared with industry benchmarks, instead of being trapped in one plant's ad-hoc naming.
Does it work if the plant floor has no reliable Wi-Fi?
Yes, if the tool is offline-first. WorkHive captures entries locally and syncs when a connection returns. Technicians never lose data on the floor, which matters for Philippine plants with patchy coverage.
