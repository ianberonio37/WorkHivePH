## Why a marketplace for industrial plants

Finding the right industrial specialist here is mostly about who you know. The plant supervisor calls a friend, who calls another friend, who knows a guy. Three days later you have a quote from someone whose work nobody has checked. Parts go the same way: panic buying from whoever has stock. That often costs 2 to 3x the planned price.

A marketplace fixes both sides. Plants get vetted suppliers and specialists with verifiable work history. Specialists get seen by plants their own network would never reach. Suppliers see what real plants need, instead of cold-calling buyers.

## How WorkHive Marketplace works

Three sides:

- **Buyers (plants):** post a need. It might be a specialist for a turbine bearing job. Or 50 units of one O-ring, or calibration of 12 transmitters. The marketplace matches a request to sellers. It uses category and location.
- **Specialist contractors:** list their services with discipline tags and certifications. Tags include rotating equipment, electrical MV, instrumentation and refrigeration. Certifications include TESDA and OEM. Note that a listing is what the seller says about themselves. The marketplace does not read your logbook and turn it into a credential. So the evidence a buyer weighs is the reviews below, not a job count.
- **Parts and equipment suppliers:** list catalogs with PHP prices and lead times. Add the minimum order and the shelf life. Plants can subscribe to recurring-consumption pulls for ABC items.

## What the trust layer actually is

The hardest problem in any industrial marketplace is trust. It is worth being exact about how much a young marketplace can carry. Two mechanisms, described as they are built:

1. **Buyer reviews.** After a job, the buyer leaves a rating from one to five stars with a comment. It is one overall score, not a breakdown by quality or speed. A review left on a completed purchase carries a verified mark. Reviews are public, and nobody can edit them afterwards. The seller gets exactly one reply to each, by design. That way, a thread cannot turn into an endless fight.
2. **Disputes with evidence.** A buyer can open a dispute, name the reason and attach evidence. That can be completion photos, delivery proof, or the listing itself. The seller replies once. Then an administrator records a decision and the date of it. Every step carries a time stamp. That is what gives the record its value later.

**What this does not give you:** the marketplace does not check a seller's work history for you. It also publishes no figures on how disputes turn out.

It has not run long enough to have honest ones. Treat the reviews as the signal they are: a handful of real opinions from people who paid. Then do the checks you would do on any contractor a friend sent you.

## How buyers and sellers connect (free, contact-only)

WorkHive Marketplace is free and does not handle payments. It is a place to find people and check them. Buyers find ID-verified specialists and suppliers. Then they contact them directly to arrange the work and the payment.

- **Buyer contacts the seller** directly. The listing gives the details: Messenger, phone or email
- **Seller delivers the work** with evidence the buyer can check. That means a logbook entry, photos and a signed handback
- **Buyer and seller settle payment between themselves**, in PHP. They pick the way: GCash, bank transfer or cheque. WorkHive never touches the money, charges no commission, and takes no cut

Invoicing and tax: the two of you pay each other directly. So you and the seller handle your own receipts and BIR compliance, as with any direct job.

## The three patterns it is built around

1. **Emergency specialist sourcing.** A bearing on a critical pump fails Friday night. The plant needs a vibration analyst on site Saturday morning. WorkHive Marketplace surfaces verified specialists within 50km radius with weekend availability. The job posts and accepts in under 2 hours. This pattern alone justifies plant adoption.
2. **Recurring consumable supply.** The plant's A-tier inventory items pull from the marketplace on autopilot. When stock hits the reorder point, ask 3 verified suppliers for a quote. The cheapest credible one wins. Plant procurement cost drops 8 to 15 percent versus the friend-call pattern.
3. **One-off project bidding.** A planned turnaround needs scaffolding, insulation, painting, and electrical contracting crews. The plant posts the scope, and verified contractors bid. The plant picks the offer that fits its budget and timeline. It can turn 3 weeks of phone and email into about 5 days of structured bidding.

## Becoming a seller

Sellers register through the marketplace with:

- Business registration (DTI or SEC) and TIN
- Discipline categories and geographic service area
- Certifications (TESDA, OEM training, PEC license, PME registration)
- Optional: WorkHive Solo-mode work history to bootstrap credibility

New listings are visible to buyers straight away. Sellers carry a tier badge that moves with the completed sales the platform records. But do not build your pitch on it. On a young marketplace, buyers read your ID check and your first few reviews. Get one real job, do it well, and ask the buyer to review it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who can sell on WorkHive Marketplace?

Specialist contractors (vibration analysts, motor rewinders, refrigeration specialists, calibration providers), equipment suppliers (pumps, seals, bearings, instrumentation), and service providers (NDT, training, project management). Sellers must be Philippine-registered businesses (DTI or SEC) with valid TIN. Individual freelance technicians can sell as sole proprietors after DTI registration.

### How is seller credibility verified?

Two things the platform can actually show you, and one it cannot. It shows whether the seller has passed ID verification - every seller card says ID-verified or NOT ID-verified, which is a more useful signal than a badge only the good ones carry. It shows buyer reviews: a rating out of five with a comment, marked verified when it sits against a completed purchase. What it does NOT do is verify the certifications a seller lists, or read their logbook and turn it into a work history - those are claims the seller makes, and worth asking them to evidence directly.

### What if there is a dispute over completed work?

You can open a dispute against the listing, giving the reason and attaching evidence such as completion photos or delivery proof. The seller gets one reply, and an administrator records a decision with the date. Be clear about the limits: WorkHive never held your money, so a decision here is a record and a judgement, not a refund - the payment was always directly between you and the seller, and recovering it is a matter between you two. We publish no statistics about how disputes turn out, because the marketplace has not run long enough to have honest ones.

### How does payment work? Is it PHP only?

WorkHive Marketplace is free and does not process payments. You contact the seller directly (Messenger, phone, or email shown on their listing) and agree the price and payment terms between yourselves, in PHP or however you both prefer (GCash, bank transfer, or cheque). WorkHive never holds funds, charges no commission, and takes no cut. You and the seller handle your own invoicing and BIR compliance directly, as with any direct engagement.

### Can buyers see the seller's prices before posting?

Yes for catalog items (equipment supplier lists with PHP prices, MOQ, lead time). For specialist services where price depends on scope, buyers post the need and receive quotes from matching sellers within 48 hours. The marketplace shows the seller's typical price range based on past jobs for context.

### What stops a buyer from gaming the review system?

Reviews are tied to completed transactions only (no buying reviews without using the service). The 7-day auto-confirm window prevents reviews from being held hostage. Sellers can respond once to a review for public context. Patterns of suspicious reviewing (same buyer leaving multiple negative reviews on competing sellers, same seller getting many 5-star reviews from accounts with no other activity) are flagged for moderation.
