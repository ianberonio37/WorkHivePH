## Seven seconds to make the cut

An applicant tracking system, or ATS, is software that filters resumes. Most employers use one before a person reads them. It parses your file into fields and scans for job titles, dates, and keywords from the job ad. Is your resume a scanned image or a styled two-column layout? Then the parser often reads garbage, and your application stops there.

Even when you pass the software, the human pass is short. Eye-tracking research clocks a recruiter's first look at about 7.4 seconds. A recruiter screening for a Batangas refinery has only seconds. In that time, they skip your paragraph about teamwork. They check your current title first. Then your previous role and dates, then your education.

## Where each resume line comes from

The builder does not invent experience. Every line traces back to something you recorded on WorkHive, or typed yourself. Knowing where each line came from makes it fast. That helps someone with years of real work who never kept a resume current.

| You recorded it as | It becomes |
| --- | --- |
| Logbook entries: faults fixed, PMs done, a 22:00 changeover repair on Conveyor #2 | Work-experience bullets with real dates |
| Skill Matrix badges: skills you earned by passing the skill exam. They are private to you; your supervisor cannot see them. | A skills section worded in job-ad terms |
| Photos of paper certificates: TESDA NCs, safety and equipment training | A certifications section, typed out by the AI |
| Anything you type or edit by hand | Stays exactly as you wrote it |

That privacy point matters, because it is what makes a badge worth putting on a resume. Only you can read your skills, badges and exam attempts. The database policy ties them to your own account, not your hive.

So you choose whom to show them to. And they stay with you after you leave the plant that trained you. The logbook entries behind your work bullets do not. Those belong to the plant’s records. Export while you are still a member.

If your plant history is thin on WorkHive, start logging now. Take a maintenance planner in Cabuyao who logs daily. Their resume updates itself. Then ✨ Polish my experience wording turns raw entries into clean bullets. It does not change the facts.

## Tailor to the job ad, not to a template

Generic resumes lose on keywords. Paste the job description into the Tailor to a job field. The builder compares your content with what the ad asks for.

It scores the match and suggests skills to bring forward. Keep only the suggestions that are true. The score tells you what to make explicit; it is not an invitation to claim work you have not done.

**Worked example:** a Davao food-plant ad asks for PM on air-handling units. Your Logbook holds 14 months of AHU-3 filter and belt PMs. Tailoring brings that history forward as a skills line and a dated bullet. Your match score shows the gap closing. Need a cover letter for the same posting? **✍ Draft a cover letter** writes one from the same evidence.

## Export ATS-plain

When the preview reads right, export with the **ATS-plain** option on. It keeps your text and strips the styling that breaks parsers:

- standard headings in a single column, no tables or graphics in the output
- plain fonts with no embedded styling for the parser to choke on
- the same wording you approved on screen, nothing reformatted behind your back

You get a PDF or Word .docx for online applications. Print is there for a walk-in at a Subic contractor. And you get a data file of your own, in the open JSON Resume format.

No website can lock it away from you. Saved versions live in 📂 My Resumes. Keep one resume per target role, and re-tailor each one in minutes.

## Five habits that beat a better-looking resume

1. **Quantify.** "Cut filler changeover downtime by 18 minutes" beats "responsible for maintenance". That holds in any shift in-charge application. Pull the numbers from your Logbook.
2. **Use the ad's own words where they are true.** ATS matching rewards keywords in context, not clever synonyms.
3. **Promote, don't duplicate.** An achievement can become a Project or an Award. Then the builder's toggle stops it from showing up again as a bullet.
4. **Keep it to two pages.** The same eye-tracking research found dense, crammed pages got skipped, not studied.
5. **Update after every project, not before every application.** Log the work when it happens and the resume is always one Auto-fill away.
