## Introduction to Asset Brain 360

Asset Brain 360 is the history page for one machine in WorkHive's Asset Hub. Scan the QR code or barcode on a machine, and its page opens. It shows the logbook entries, PM history, parts used and project work on one timeline. So decisions about that machine rest on its real record.

Take a bottling-line pump, P-204B, at a plant in Cabuyao, Calabarzon. Scan its tag, and you see every job logged against it, with the downtime your team recorded. A breadcrumb above the name shows the equipment it belongs to. The hierarchy follows ISO 14224 asset and failure coding.

The page also shows the asset's live state, its own **Risk Profile** and **Ask Asset Brain**, a question box. Fleet-wide risk lives elsewhere. Asset Hub's asset list carries a risk band for every machine. Analytics has the failure-trend forecast.

The asset page answers “how is this machine?”. The asset list answers “which machine first?”. You can start at zero cost. Print QR labels, stick them on, start logging, and the history builds itself.

## Scanning a Machine's QR Code

Open Asset Hub and tap **Scan**. Your phone's camera opens. Point it at the label on the machine. The scanner reads QR codes and common barcodes: Code 128, EAN-13 and Code 39.

If you block the camera, or the decoder will not load, the scanner stays open. You can type the tag in. Type the tag instead. That path still works when the decoder cannot load on a weak signal.

1. Open the Asset Hub page in WorkHive.
2. Tap **Scan** to open your camera.
3. Scan the label on the machine, such as pump P-204B.
4. The machine's page opens with its full timeline.
5. If the tag is not in your register yet, the scan does not fail quietly. It puts what it read into the search box, so you can see what was on the label.

## The Full Per-Asset Timeline

The timeline puts a machine's whole record in date order. It holds logbook entries, PM history, parts used and project work. Around it sit the parent breadcrumb, the Risk Profile and Ask Asset Brain.

**Ask Asset Brain** answers from this machine, its parent and its recent timeline. It also checks failures on peer machines of the same equipment class in your hive. Each answer cites the records it used.

Below these sits the part most readers miss: the Reliability Workbench. It has five tabs of analysis on the same asset:

- An FMEA (failure mode and effects analysis) matrix, per AIAG-VDA 2019
- An RCM (reliability-centered maintenance) strategy, per SAE JA1011 and JA1012
- A Weibull fit, per IEC 61649
- P-F intervals, per ISO 13381-1
- The last 365 days of corrective history

**Worked example:** A bottling line in Cabuyao runs around the clock, and pump P-204B stops. To price that stop, you need two facts. How often has it happened, and how long did each one take to clear? That is the timeline's job.

Scan the pump's tag, and its jobs and the downtime your team logged are already there. Use your line's own cost per hour, not a figure from an article.

## Machine Relationships and Parts Information

The page also shows how the machine relates to others. A breadcrumb above its name shows its parent: the line, the skid or the system. So a technician can place the pump at a glance. The chain is one level today, and deeper links are still being built.

Parts appear in two ways. First, every part used on the machine shows in the timeline. You can read each one back. Second, a card suggests parts to stage. It appears once the model has enough history. It suggests parts to set aside before the job, with a confidence chip. You accept it or ignore it.

The first records what you used. The second is a forecast of what to have ready. Read the parent chain and the parts record together. They show whether a machine fails on its own, or because of what it is attached to.

## The Risk Profile, and Where Fleet Risk Lives

The **Risk Profile** card gives the asset a band: critical, high, medium or low. It comes from the maintenance history your team logged. A batch job works it out daily at 13:00 PHT. So you see yesterday's evidence, not a live reading. If a pump is too new, or the job has not run yet, the card says so. It does not make up a score.

Comparing machines is a different view. Looking for it on the asset page is the most common way to get lost. Asset Hub's asset list shows every machine's risk band, so the riskiest stand out. Analytics' Predictive phase holds the failure-trend forecast.

A rules engine scores assets from day one. A GBM model retrains every week. Until the hive has 500 repair records, its training report warns that it is not yet reliable.

| Risk Level | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Critical | Act now: this is the asset the shift plan should open with |
| High | Act soon to prevent failure |
| Medium | Watch closely and schedule maintenance |
| Low | Running within normal limits |

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Asset Brain 360?

It is the history page for one machine in WorkHive Asset Hub. One QR scan opens the machine's whole record.

### How do I access Asset Brain 360?

Open Asset Hub and tap Scan, then scan the QR code or barcode on the machine. You can also search for the machine by name or tag.

### What kind of information is available in Asset Brain 360?

Its timeline, its parent breadcrumb, its own Risk Profile band, the parts issued against it, Ask Asset Brain, and the Reliability Workbench (FMEA, RCM, Weibull, P-F).

### Can I use Asset Brain 360 for machines that are not in our current asset register?

No. A machine needs a record in Asset Hub first. If you scan a tag that is not registered, the scanner puts what it read into the search box, so you can see the label and add the machine.

### Is Asset Brain 360 available for all types of machines?

Yes, for any machine registered in Asset Hub. A QR label makes it one scan away, but search works without one.

### Can I customize the information displayed in Asset Brain 360?

No, the information displayed in Asset Brain 360 is standardized based on ISO 14224 asset hierarchy and failure coding.
